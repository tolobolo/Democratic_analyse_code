
1. The Creator & NameSystem: Majority Judgment (Le Jugement Majoritaire).   Creators: Michel Balinski and Rida Laraki, two French researchers and mathematicians based in Paris.   2. How the System WorksInstead of picking just one candidate (First-Past-The-Post) or putting candidates in numerical order (Ranked-Choice), voters assign an independent quality grade to every party:$$\text{Excellent} \quad > \quad \text{Very Good} \quad > \quad \text{Good} \quad > \quad \text{Acceptable} \quad > \quad \text{Poor} \quad > \quad \text{Reject}$$Finding the Winner: The system collects all the grades for a party, sorts them, and finds the Median Grade (the exact middle vote at the 50% mark). The party with the highest median wins.   Breaking Ties: If two parties share the same median grade (e.g., both get a median of Good), the system checks who has more votes strictly above or fewer strictly below the median to determine the winner.3. Key Findings from Our DiscussionWhy "Reject" Matters: Reject is the absolute lowest grade. It pulls down polarizing candidates who are loved by some but hated by many, favoring broader consensus candidates who are widely accepted.Does Dirty Voting / "Bullet Voting" Break It?If a group of voters gives their favorite Excellent and everyone else Reject, it does not break the median unless that dirty group makes up over 50% of all voters.If a critical mass of voters plays dirty, the system turns into a basic "us vs. them" headcount, but it still protects moderate options better than traditional single-vote systems.No Split Votes: Two similar parties (e.g., two center-left parties) don't steal votes from each other or hand the win to an opposing party—voters can simply give both parties high grades without penalizing either.Impact on Radical Policies: Because candidates need a high median score across the whole population to win, extreme candidates are forced to soften their stances, leading to smoother, less radical policy changes over time.4. How to Test It in CodeNo "Magic Numbers" Needed: You don't need numerical scores or averages. Grades have a natural sequence (Excellent > Poor > Reject), so a sorted list or an Enum in code handles the evaluation.Testing Reality: To see how the system holds up, test scenarios with different voter groups—such as Honest Voters vs. Strategic Attackers or Polarized Left/Right Voters vs. Centrist Compromise Voters.


5. Code
All code is in election.py, tests in test_election.py (pytest). Grades are the Grade Enum with no numeric values; their order is the declaration order in GRADES_DESC (best first). A ballot is dict[candidate, Grade]; a missing grade counts as Reject (handled in grades_for only). Median: sort a candidate's grades best to worst and take the middle one. Even count: if the two middle grades are equal that is the median, otherwise drop the worst grade and try again. Ties between parties with the same median: the party with more grades above the median wins. If still tied, remove the worst grade from both parties and start over (median, then count above, then remove worst again). Numbers are never used, neither to generate grades nor to evaluate them.

Voter model (no numbers, no hidden logic; real people do not follow a formula):
- Every voter picks one random favourite party (Excellent) and gives every other party a random grade from Very Good down to Reject (random.choice). The favourite is the voter's single vote under plurality and Storting.
- There is no honest/strategic split any more; it was removed because it did not fit the one-vote systems.
- Do not reintroduce latent quality scores, positions or noise. Numbers were removed on purpose.

Districts and mandates: voters are split over the 19 Storting districts (DISTRICTS table, official 2025 seat counts, 169 total, population 1.1.2024 for the voter split). Each district gives up one mandate (seats_in = official - 1, 150 in total); the 19 given up are the "Utjevning" mandates. Every system is counted per district so all three hand out seats:
- Majority Judgment: MJ ranking per district, then split_seats gives the district's mandates to the winner (--split 1, default) or shares them among the top parties by weights (--split 2,1,1 = half/quarter/quarter, --split 3,2,1 = most/ok/least). Largest-remainder rounding, parties beyond the weights get nothing. --split norway (NORWAY) instead shares each district's mandates by modified Sainte-Lague exactly like the Storting count, on supporters = voters who graded the party MJ_SUPPORT_GRADE (Good) or better, because grades carry no vote count. The split setting applies to Majority Judgment only. Report order everywhere: Majority Judgment, Storting, Plurality. Under norway, MJ's levelling fair share and 4% threshold are also measured in supporters (mj_support over all ballots), never in votes. Purpose: make the mandate split identical so only the voting system differs.
- Plurality: first past the post, the party with most votes takes all of the district's mandates. The split setting never applies (one vote per ballot for the favourite, collect_votes called once per district and shared with Storting).
- Storting (Norway): modified Sainte-Lague (sainte_lague, divisors 1.4, 3, 5, 7, ...) per district with that district's seats_in. Not D'Hondt. The split switch never applies here.
Sperregrense switch (--sperregrense / --no-sperregrense, UI radio):
- ON (default): the 19 Utjevning mandates are levelling seats (levelling_seats), Norway's deficit rule, applied the same way to all three systems: eligible = parties with at least THRESHOLD (4%) of the national vote (falls back to all if none). Fair share = modified Sainte-Lague of the seats not held by ineligible parties, on national votes. A party whose district seats already exceed its fair share is removed and the fair share recomputed without it. Each remaining party gets fair share minus district seats; these sum to exactly 19. The split rule never applies to the pool, so no party can take all 19.
- OFF: exactly the same levelling rule, but every party is eligible regardless of vote share. (An earlier version made Utjevning a 20th district with its own voters; that removed the levelling effect and was dropped on 2026-11-02.)
count_all returns an Election with national seats, per-district seats and the largest party per system. The old single-pool helpers (plurality_from_votes, storting_from_votes) remain for tests only.

Real voters (CSES): data/cses_imd_csv.zip is the CSES Integrated Module Dataset (not in git, data/ is ignored; CSES terms forbid redistribution). --cses YEAR (1997, 2001, 2005, 2009, 2013, 2017) or the UI "Voters" selector loads Norwegian respondents. First use writes data/cses_norway.csv (only Norway rows and needed columns) so later runs are fast. Mapping: 0-10 like/dislike score -> grade via SCORE_TO_GRADE (9-10 Excellent, 7-8 Very Good, 5-6 Good, 3-4 Acceptable, 1-2 Poor, 0 Reject; the asymmetry for Reject is accepted). Missing/refused answers count as Reject; party slots nobody rated that year are dropped. The respondent's reported lower-house vote (IMD3002_LH_PL) is the plurality/Storting vote; if missing or for a party not rated that year, the highest-rated party is used (random among ties). Respondents are spread over the districts at random in proportion to population (every district gets at least one). CSES_PARTIES maps codes 5780001-5780010 to Ap, Høyre, Frp, SV, MDG, Sp, KrF, Venstre, Rødt, Kystpartiet; names were inferred from vote shares, confirm against the CSES codebook if exactness matters.

Run: pytest, then python election.py --voters 10000 --split 2,1,1 --no-sperregrense (default parties: Far-left, Center, Far-right); python election.py --cses 2017

UI: python3 ui.py serves board.html and two endpoints: GET /api/presets and POST /api/run (JSON: {"voters": {"source": "random"|"cses_YEAR", "parties": [...], "n": N, "seed": S}, "systems": [SystemSpec dicts]}). ui.py only moves JSON (handle_run is the pure function behind the endpoint, with a small cache of electorates keyed by the voter settings); all counting lives in election.py. board.html is one static file (HTML, CSS, vanilla JS, no build step): a sidebar with the shared voter settings, a toolbar with "New system" and preset buttons, and draggable blocks, one per system, re-run on every change (no run button). Block positions and specs persist in localStorage ("board.v1"). Dark mode via CSS variables. UI_PORT env var changes the port; tests use 8765 so they never touch a running UI on 8000.

Systems API (election.py): SystemSpec(name, method, split, levelling). method "grades" = Majority Judgment ranking (p and q shown), "votes" = one vote per voter. split = "1" | "2,1,1" | "3,2,1" | "norway" | custom weights; applies to both methods (votes + norway = the Storting count). levelling = "threshold" (4% sperregrense) | "open" | "none" (all 169 mandates stay in the districts, seats_in(d, False)). PRESETS: Norwegian (votes, norway, threshold), American (votes, 1, none). Only real-world systems are presets; Majority Judgment blocks are created through "New system". make_voters/build_voters create a Voters object (ballots + votes per district, with cached per-district MJ rankings and supporter counts); run_system(spec, voters) -> SeatsResult; run_board(voters, specs) -> JSON-ready dict. count_all and the CLI are thin wrappers that run the three default systems through run_system. Ranking uses mj_key (per-party key from grade counts; one (median rank, -above) per removal round), which is provably the same order as the pairwise compare() (tested on random inputs) but O(n) per party instead of O(n^2) per pair.
